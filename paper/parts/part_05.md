Nevertheless, the regulatory and operational constraints
must also be satisfied but ensuring feasibility is already a
hard problem. This study considers the following set of 7
essential constraints for operation and safety. A description of
their derivation and significance with regards to safety can be
found in the website of the United States Nuclear Regulatory
Commission (NRC) (e.g., for peaking factors [52]):

1. c1 : Ensuring a lower limit for the cycle length to respect
the refueling outage schedule, where c1 ≥ 500 EFPD is
chosen as a constraint.
2. c2 : An upper limit on the peak pin peaking factor Fq ,
which is a safety parameter measuring the ratio between
the maximum local power produced to the average power
generated by the fuel rods in the core. It is monitored to
limit the fuel centerline temperature and Pellet-Cladding
concerns. The constraint is c2 ≤ 1.85.
3. c3 : An upper limit of the rod-integrated power F h , which
is a safety parameter measuring the maximum total power
generated by a fuel rod. It is monitored in particular to
respect the margin to departure from nucleate boiling
ratio. The constraint is c3 ≤ 1.45.
4. c4 : To help maintain the core critical, diluted Boron in
the coolant is used in PWRs but it must be limited due to
safety reasons. In particular, we consider an upper limit
of the boron concentration Cb to control the axial offset
due to boron deposition and prevent positive temperature
coefficient of reactivity. The constraint is set to c4 ≤ 1200
ppm.
5. c5 : An upper limit of the pin peak discharge burnup Bu max
for fuel performance concerns (e.g., hydrogen buildup).
The constraint is c5 ≤ 62 GWd/tHm (Giga Watt day per
ton of Heavy Metal).
6. c6 and c7 : Limits of the number of different enrichment (c6 ) and BP (c7 ) patterns in fresh fuel loading
batches, respectively. The constraints are 2 ≤ c6 ≤ 3,
1 ≤ c7 ≤ 3. The upper bounds correspond to potential constraints imposed by the fuel vendors. The lower
bounds are assumed based on expert judgment to enable
effective reduction of power peaking in the core.
These constraints are called instantaneous [53] because
they must be satisfied after one action. They are also dubbed
implicit [53] because they are obtained with a 3D nodal
nuclear depletion simulation code SIMULATE3 [20]. In
literature, three main categories of objective function are utilized namely penalty function-, step-wise or piece-wise-, and
ratio-based methods [54]. Here a penalty-based method was
founds to work better. The LCOE and the constraints are
